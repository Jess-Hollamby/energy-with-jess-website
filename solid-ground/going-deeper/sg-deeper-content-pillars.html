Content Pillars Index

A starting point. Pick 3-4 that feel true for you and build from there.

How to use this. A content pillar is a theme that's always true about your work - something you could talk about every week without running dry. Think of it as a lane, not a single topic. "How I work" is a pillar. "Why I always start a session in silence" is a post that lives inside it. Most practitioners need 3-4 pillars. Once you have them, you'll always know where to start. Add your own at the bottom of each section.
Pillar group 01

Your Practice

What you do, how you do it, and why it works. The most practical and trust-building pillar group.

Pillar
How I work
Your philosophy, your approach, your non-negotiables. This is where people decide whether they trust you before they ever book.
Example posts
  • Why I always start a session in silence
  • What I won't do in a treatment room (and why)
  • The thing I tell every new client before we begin
  • Why I keep my sessions to a set length
Pillar
What a session actually involves
Demystify the experience. Most people have never had a session - they don't know what happens, what to expect, or what to wear. Answering these questions is content.
Example posts
  • What to expect in your first Reiki session
  • What I'm doing when I work with your energy field
  • Why you might feel emotional after a session
  • The most common question I get asked before a booking
Pillar
What happens after a session
The integration piece - what the body and energy do after the work is done. Underrated and high-value content.
Example posts
  • Why you might feel tired after a treatment (it's not a bad sign)
  • What I recommend for the 24 hours after a session
  • The shift that happens slowly, not all at once
  • Signs the work is moving through you
Gut check If you could only post about one of these three for a month, which one would you choose? Start there.
Pillar group 02

Your Modality

Education builds authority without you having to claim it. Teach what you know - people follow practitioners who explain things clearly.

Pillar
What [your modality] actually is
Not the Wikipedia version. Your version - what it is, what it does, and who it's for. Write this in the words your clients use, not the words you learned in training.
Example posts
  • What Reiki actually is (from someone who does it every day)
  • The difference between relaxation massage and remedial - and how to know which one you need
  • What people get wrong about energy healing
  • What I say when someone asks me to explain what I do at a dinner party
Pillar
The questions people are afraid to ask
Every practitioner has a list of questions they hear constantly. Answer them in public - it signals safety and saves the person Googling at midnight.
Example posts
  • Does Reiki work if you don't believe in it?
  • Can massage make anxiety worse?
  • What if I cry during a session?
  • Do I have to talk during a session or can I just be quiet?
Pillar
Who this work is for
Specificity attracts. The more clearly you describe who your work is for, the more those people recognise themselves.
Example posts
  • Who I see most often in my treatment room (and what they all have in common)
  • Signs your nervous system is asking for something different
  • This isn't for everyone - but it might be for you
  • The person who gets the most out of this work
Pillar group 03

The Practitioner Behind the Practice

People book people, not services. Sharing who you are - without oversharing - builds the kind of trust that turns a follower into a client.

Pillar
Why I do this work
Your origin story - the moment, the realisation, or the slow accumulation of experiences that led you here. You don't have to share everything. Share what's true and what's relevant.
Example posts
  • What made me choose this over everything else
  • The moment I knew this was the right path
  • What I was doing before this (and why I left)
  • The experience that changed how I approach my work
Pillar
What I'm still learning
Continuing education, professional development, what you're currently studying or exploring. This signals commitment to your craft and models what it looks like to keep growing.
Example posts
  • What I'm studying right now and why
  • The course that changed how I practice
  • What I thought I knew about X before I learned Y
  • The thing I wish I'd been taught in training
Pillar
My own practice and healing
What you do to stay regulated, grounded, and resourced as a practitioner. Optional - share only what you're genuinely comfortable with. Authenticity always over vulnerability performance.
Example posts
  • What I do after a heavy session
  • My non-negotiables for staying well in this work
  • What I've had to unlearn to take better care of myself
  • The practice I return to when everything else falls away
Pillar group 04

Holding Space + Client Care

The relational side of your work. What it feels like to be in your care - before, during, and after.

Pillar
How I prepare for a session
Your rituals, your setup, the way you arrive for a client. This is gold for trust - it shows your work has intention behind it.
Example posts
  • What I do before every client arrives
  • Why I don't book back-to-back sessions
  • The 10 minutes before a session that change everything
  • How I clear my space (and my own energy) between clients
Pillar
Boundaries in practice
What you hold and what you don't. This content attracts clients who respect you and filters out the ones who won't.
Example posts
  • What I do when a session stays with me after the client leaves
  • Why I have a late cancellation policy (and what it protects)
  • The boundary I had to learn the hard way
  • When I refer a client out - and why that's part of good care
Pillar group 05

The Solo Business Reality

The honest, unglamorous truth of running a practice alone. This content builds a community around you - other practitioners and future clients who appreciate realness.

Pillar
What solo practice actually looks like
The admin, the in-between, the deciding everything yourself. Normalise this - it's what most practitioners experience and nobody talks about.
Example posts
  • What a Tuesday looks like when you work for yourself
  • The part of running a practice nobody warned me about
  • What I do on a week with no bookings
  • The decision I had to make completely alone (and how)
Pillar
Pricing and being paid
One of the most-searched topics for practitioners. Your honest take on pricing, valuing your work, and getting comfortable asking for money.
Example posts
  • How I decided what to charge for my first service
  • What changed when I raised my prices
  • Why "I can't afford that" doesn't always mean what we think
  • The conversation about money I had to have with myself first
Pillar
How I found my first clients
Practical and vulnerable. Every practitioner starts somewhere - and most people think it was easier for everyone else than it was for them.
Example posts
  • My first paying client - what I charged and how nervous I was
  • What actually worked when I was starting out
  • What didn't work (and what I do differently now)
  • Where most of my clients have come from over the years
Pillar group 06

Seasonal + Cyclical

Content that maps to natural rhythms - seasonal, lunar, or annual. Evergreen structure with fresh angles every cycle.

Pillar
What the body needs in [season]
Seasonal body wisdom gives you four built-in content angles a year - and it's endlessly relevant because the seasons always come back.
Example posts
  • What winter is asking of your nervous system
  • The seasonal shift I see most in my treatment room right now
  • What to do with your body in the transition between seasons
  • Why [season] is the hardest time of year for sensitive people
Pillar
Rest as a practice
For you and for your clients. Rest is countercultural and deeply relevant - especially for the people who come to practitioners because they've been running on empty.
Example posts
  • What I've learned about rest from watching my clients
  • The kind of rest that actually restores you
  • Why I build rest into my business - not just my life
  • What happens in the body when you finally stop

Pick your three.

Scroll back through and mark the pillars that feel most like you. Pick one from at least two different groups. You're not committing forever - you're starting. Pillars shift as your practice does.
