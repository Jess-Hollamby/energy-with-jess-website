Welcome Email Template

The first email your new subscribers receive. Warm, clear, and personal - this sets the tone for everything that comes after.

Your welcome email is the highest-opened email you will ever send. People have just signed up and they're curious. This is the moment to make them feel like they made a good decision. Keep it real, keep it warm, make it feel like a message from a person - not a business.

Subject Line Options

Pick one. The simplest options often perform best - familiar, human, no tricks.
  • Welcome - you're in. Direct, warm, confident
  • Hello from [your name] Personal, low-pressure
  • Here's your [freebie name] Delivery-focused, clear expectation
  • You just made a good decision. Affirming, confident
  • Nice to meet you. Conversational, human

Tips for Your Welcome Email

The most important thing

Sound like a person, not a brand. Read it back out loud before you send it. If it sounds like a corporate newsletter, rewrite it as if you're texting a friend you haven't spoken to in a while.

Keep it short

People skim welcome emails. Three or four short paragraphs is enough. You'll have time to go deeper in future emails.

Deliver what you promised

If someone signed up for a freebie, the freebie must be in this email or immediately accessible. Don't make them wait or search.

Set an expectation

Tell them how often they'll hear from you. People unsubscribe because they don't know what's coming. One line is enough.

Invite a reply

Ask one question or just say "hit reply if you want to say hi." Replies tell your email provider this is a real conversation, which helps deliverability.

One soft CTA only

Don't ask people to follow you, book, and share all in one email. One gentle next step is enough. Booking link is the most useful one at this stage.

Send it yourself first

Before you go live, send a test to yourself. Check the links work, the freebie attaches, and it reads correctly on mobile. Most people read email on their phone.
