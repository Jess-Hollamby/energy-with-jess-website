Solid Ground - Module 5

Where Your First Clients Come From

They're closer than you think. Start here, not on a cold Instagram grid.

Your first clients are almost never strangers. They're people who already know you, trust you, or have seen your name somewhere. This exercise helps you find them - and gives you the words to reach out.
The Three Circles

Write as many names as you can in each circle. Don't overthink it. Your first clients are almost certainly in Circles 1 and 2.

Outreach Scripts

One for each circle. These are starting points - adjust the language so it sounds like you, not a template.

Circle 1

People who know you

Close friends, family,
existing contacts

These people already trust you. You don't need to sell - just tell them what you're doing and ask them to pass it on. Keep it personal and direct.

Text / DM
Hey [name], I wanted to let you know I've officially started taking clients with [business name]. I'm offering [what you do, e.g. Reiki sessions online and in-person]. If you know anyone who might be interested, I'd love it if you passed on my details. Here's my booking link if you ever want to try a session yourself: [link]
In person
"I've just started [business name] - I'm doing [brief description]. I'm building my client base at the moment so if you know anyone who might be keen, send them my way."
  • Don't apologise for reaching out. You're sharing something, not imposing.
  • Personalise each message - one line that shows you thought of them specifically goes a long way.
  • You don't need a big announcement. A direct message to five people beats a vague post to 500.
Circle 2

People who know of you

Acquaintances, former colleagues,
social media connections

These people recognise your name but may not know what you do now. You're reconnecting and letting them know. Keep it warm, not salesy - you're informing, not pitching.

DM or email
Hi [name], hope you're well. I've recently started [business name] - I offer [brief description, e.g. Reiki and energy healing for women who need to slow down]. I'm just getting started and building my client base, so if you or anyone you know has been curious about [what you do], I'd love to help. You can see what I offer and book here: [link]
Comment reply or story mention
Thanks so much - and yes, I've just launched [business name]! I do [what you do]. If you ever want to know more, my link is in my bio.
  • A brief "hope you're well" acknowledgment goes further than jumping straight to business.
  • If you haven't spoken in a while, lead with connection - not your offer.
  • One clear link is enough. Don't list everything you do.
Circle 3

Strangers

Public posts, new followers,
online communities

You're not trying to close a sale here - you're making yourself visible and letting people know you exist. These posts plant seeds. Results take longer, but they're worth doing alongside the direct outreach above.

Instagram / Facebook caption
I've been quiet about this for a while, but I'm officially open for bookings. I'm [your name] and I offer [what you do] for [who it's for]. If you've been curious about [service] - or you know someone who could use it - now's a good time to reach out. [Link in bio / DM me / Book here: link]
Community / Facebook group post
Hi everyone - I'm [name], based in [location]. I've just launched [business name] - I offer [what you do] [online / in-person / both]. If you have questions or want to know more, feel free to message me. Happy to chat.
  • Post before you feel ready. "Almost ready" is not a reason to wait.
  • Being personal in your caption - even a little vulnerable - outperforms polished every time.
  • One clear CTA at the end: link in bio, DM me, or a direct booking link. Pick one.
  • Consistency matters more than the perfect post. Show up regularly and let people find you.
Send the messages first. Post to strangers second. Your first client is almost always someone who already knows you - and they're waiting to hear from you.
