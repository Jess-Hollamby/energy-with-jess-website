Content Pillars Index

For product-based businesses. A starting point - pick 3-4 and build from there.

How to use this. A content pillar is a theme that's always true about your work - something you could post about every week without running dry. It's a lane, not a single topic. "How I make this" is a pillar. "The reason I switched to natural dyes last year" is a post that lives inside it. Most product businesses need 3-4 pillars. Once you have them, you'll always know where to start. Add your own at the bottom of each section.
Pillar group 01

Your Product

What it is, what it does, and why someone needs it in their life. The most direct trust-builder you have.

Pillar
What it is and what it does
Not the spec sheet version - the human version. What does someone actually get when they buy this? What does it change, solve, or add to their life? Say it simply. Say it often.
Example posts
  • What [product] actually does (in plain language)
  • The problem I made this to solve
  • What my customers tell me they notice first
  • Why I designed it this way and not any other
Pillar
How to use it
Usage content removes the friction between "I'm interested" and "I'll buy it." Show how it fits into a real life, not an aspirational one. This is evergreen and endlessly repurposable.
Example posts
  • How I use this in my own daily routine
  • Three ways to use [product] you might not have tried
  • What to do with it the first time it arrives
  • The most common mistake people make when using it (and the fix)
Pillar
What makes it different
Not "better than the competition" - you don't need to name anyone. Just what's true about yours. The material, the method, the intention behind it. Specificity builds trust faster than any claim.
Example posts
  • Why I chose [ingredient / material / method] and not the cheaper alternative
  • What I won't compromise on in this product
  • The detail most people don't notice (but I'll never change)
  • Why the version I sell is made differently from what you'd find in a shop
Gut check If you only had one post to explain your product to a stranger, what would you say? Write that. Then post it.
Pillar group 02

How It's Made

Process content is underused by most product businesses. It builds authority, explains your pricing, and makes people feel connected to what they're buying.

Pillar
Behind the scenes of making it
What does it actually take to make this? The steps, the time, the decisions along the way. Most customers have no idea - and most makers forget that. Show them.
Example posts
  • What goes into making one [product] (step by step)
  • The part of the process that takes longer than people expect
  • What I'm doing when you see my workspace is a mess
  • A day in the making - what it actually looks like to run this solo
Pillar
What goes into it
Ingredients, materials, sourcing, suppliers - the inputs that make your product what it is. This is especially powerful when you make considered choices others don't. Sharing it explains your price without having to justify it.
Example posts
  • Where I source [main ingredient or material] and why it matters
  • What I looked for before I found the supplier I use now
  • Why I use [material] instead of the cheaper version
  • What I refuse to put in this product - and what I use instead
Pillar
The decisions I make that you never see
The invisible quality choices - the things you do that a customer would never know about unless you told them. These posts build deep trust and distinguish a thoughtful maker from a generic seller.
Example posts
  • Why I reject a batch when [thing happens] - even if it looks fine
  • The standard I hold everything to before it leaves my hands
  • The time I remade an entire run because something wasn't right
  • What I check that no one else will ever see
Pillar group 03

The Person Behind the Product

People buy from people. Not from shops. Sharing who you are - without oversharing - builds the kind of connection that turns a browser into a loyal customer.

Pillar
Why I started making this
Your origin story. The gap you saw, the need you felt, the moment you decided to make something instead of waiting for someone else to. You don't have to share everything - share what's true and what connects.
Example posts
  • What I was doing before this and why I left it
  • The problem I couldn't find an answer to - so I made one
  • The moment I decided to start selling instead of just making
  • What I thought would happen when I launched (and what actually did)
Pillar
Running this solo
The honest, unglamorous truth of doing everything yourself. This content normalises the reality of solo business and builds a community of people who get it - including your future customers, who respect you more for it.
Example posts
  • What a busy week looks like when it's just me
  • The part of running a product business nobody warned me about
  • What I do when an order goes wrong and there's nobody to call
  • The decision I had to make completely alone (and how I got there)
Pillar
What this work means to me
The meaning behind the making. Not in a performed way - in a real one. Why this product, why this work, why you keep going on the hard weeks. Optional - share only what you genuinely mean.
Example posts
  • The part of this work I didn't expect to love
  • What making this has taught me about myself
  • Why I'm still here after [X] years / [X] hard months
  • What I want people to feel when they use this
Pillar group 04

Your Customer

The more clearly you describe who your product is for, the more those people recognise themselves - and feel like it was made for them. Because it was.

Pillar
Who buys this and why
Describe your customer in their own language. Where they are in life, what they were looking for, what they felt when they found your product. Specificity here is magnetic - it doesn't narrow your audience, it focuses it.
Example posts
  • Who I made this for (and who keeps surprising me by buying it)
  • The person who gets the most out of this product
  • What people are usually dealing with when they find me
  • The thing my customers all seem to have in common
Pillar
What changes after they buy it
The after - what's different in someone's life, routine, or how they feel once they have this product. Not a testimonial pitch. Just the honest story of what this product changes.
Example posts
  • What customers tell me after they've been using it for a month
  • The feedback I didn't expect to get (and why it meant a lot)
  • What I hope someone feels the first time they use this
  • The message I got from a customer that I still think about
Pillar
Who this isn't for
Saying who your product isn't for is one of the most trusted things you can do. It signals confidence. It also filters out the wrong buyers - which saves you both time and disappointment.
Example posts
  • Who this product isn't the right fit for (and what I'd suggest instead)
  • The question I always ask before recommending this to someone
  • When I'd tell someone to wait before buying
  • What this product won't do - so you know what it will
Pillar group 05

Education + Context

Teach people something true and useful that connects to your product. Education builds authority without you having to claim it - and it keeps people coming back even when they're not ready to buy yet.

Pillar
What I know about [your topic] that most people don't
The knowledge behind your product. What do you understand about your category - ingredients, materials, methods, the industry - that your customer would find genuinely useful? Share the real stuff.
Example posts
  • What to actually look for when buying [product type] (from someone who makes it)
  • The industry standard I don't follow - and why
  • What the label doesn't tell you about [ingredient / material]
  • Three things I'd want anyone to know before they buy [product type]
Pillar
Pricing and being paid
One of the most-searched topics for product sellers. Your honest take on what things actually cost, what goes into your prices, and what it means to value your work properly.
Example posts
  • What it actually costs to make [product] - and what that means for the price
  • Why I don't compete on price (and what I do instead)
  • The conversation I had to have with myself before I set my prices
  • What changed when I stopped undercharging
Pillar
Common questions and misconceptions
Every product has a list of questions that come up again and again. Answer them in public - it saves the person Googling at midnight and signals you have nothing to hide.
Example posts
  • The most common question I get asked about this product (and the real answer)
  • What people think this does vs. what it actually does
  • Why [common belief about your product type] isn't quite true
  • The FAQ I wish I'd written sooner
Pillar group 06

Seasons, Launches + New Things

Time-pegged content gives your audience a reason to keep watching. Seasonal rhythms, new releases, and behind-the-scenes of what's coming next.

Pillar
What's coming
The build-up. New products, seasonal editions, restocks, changes you're making. Sharing the process before launch builds anticipation and gets people invested before anything is available to buy.
Example posts
  • Something new is coming - here's where I'm at with it
  • The product I've been working on for [X] months
  • What I'm testing right now (no promises yet)
  • I'm changing [thing] - here's why
Pillar
Seasonal and gifting content
Four built-in content cycles a year - and gifting seasons on top of that. Seasonal content is evergreen in structure, fresh in angle, and practically useful for your audience.
Example posts
  • What I'd give someone who's hard to buy for (and actually means it)
  • Why [season] is when I sell most of [product] - and who's buying it
  • What I make in [season] that I don't stock any other time of year
  • The [season] edit: what I'd pick from my own range if I could only choose three

Pick your three.

Scroll back through and mark the pillars that feel most like you. Pick one from at least two different groups. You're not committing forever - you're starting. Pillars shift as your business does.
