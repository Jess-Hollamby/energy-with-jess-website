What Nobody Tells You About Psychic Readings

What actually happens in a reading - and why it matters.

← Back to Blog
By Jess Hollamby October 2026 6 min read

Most people who book their first psychic reading expect to be told the future.

They come in with a question - usually something about a relationship, a decision, a direction - and they want an answer. A clear one, ideally. Something they can take away and act on.

That's not quite what happens in a reading. And the thing that does happen is, in my experience, considerably more useful.

What a reading actually does

A psychic or tarot reading doesn't predict a fixed future. The future isn't fixed - and any reader who tells you otherwise is, at best, oversimplifying.

What a reading does is show you the energy of where you currently are. What's active. What's building. What patterns are running. What you've been circling that you haven't quite looked at directly. Sometimes that information points clearly in one direction. Often it illuminates something you already knew but hadn't let yourself fully see.

That's the part that surprises people. The reading doesn't tell them something new so much as it names something they've been carrying quietly. And there's something about having it reflected back clearly - in a space that's entirely about them, with no agenda - that moves things.

The things people actually get from readings

  • Clarity on a decision they've been going back and forth on for too long
  • Permission to trust what they already knew
  • A sense of what's underneath a situation - the energy driving it, not just the surface
  • The feeling of being genuinely seen, sometimes for the first time in a while
  • Confirmation that they're not imagining what they've been sensing
  • A starting point - a thread to pull, a direction to move

That last one is probably the most common thing I hear: "I didn't know where to start." A reading gives you that. It doesn't decide for you. But it shows you where to look.

The fear that usually comes first

A lot of people put off their first reading because they're a little bit scared of what they might hear.

That's worth naming directly. The fear usually sounds like: what if it's bad news? Or: what if I find out I'm about to make the wrong choice? Or something more specific and more personal that they haven't said out loud yet.

My experience is that readings don't tend to land like bad news. They land like information. Sometimes that information asks something of you - to look at something honestly, to consider a different direction, to acknowledge something you've been avoiding. But it doesn't arrive as a verdict. It arrives as a conversation.

And you're always the one who decides what to do with it.

Who readings are for

People assume readings are for moments of crisis. You're at a crossroads, you've had a shock, you need guidance. And yes - those are good times for a reading.

But some of the most useful readings I've done have been for people who are technically fine. They're not in crisis. They're just a little bit stuck. A little bit flat. A little bit disconnected from themselves. And the reading helps them reconnect.

If any of these sound familiar, a reading is probably a good starting point:

  • You feel like something is off but can't pinpoint it
  • You're facing a decision and keep going in circles
  • You have a sense something is about to shift and want to understand it
  • You want to understand a pattern that keeps repeating in your life
  • You've been feeling disconnected from yourself and want to find the thread back
  • You're curious - no specific reason, just a pull toward it

That last one is a valid reason. A pull toward something is worth following.

What to expect from a session with me

I read psychically and with tarot. I don't require a list of questions - though if you have them, bring them. We can move through specific areas or let it open and see what comes up. Both approaches work.

Sessions are calm and grounded. I don't do dramatic reveals or predictions designed to keep you coming back. What I aim for is something real and useful - something you can actually work with after you leave.

Most people feel lighter by the end of a session than they did at the start. Not because I've fixed anything - but because being genuinely seen tends to do that.
