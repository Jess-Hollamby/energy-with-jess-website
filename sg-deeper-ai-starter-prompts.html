AI Starter Prompts

Ready-to-use prompts for common solo business tasks. Copy, adapt, and make them yours.

How to use this. Copy any prompt into Claude, ChatGPT, or whichever AI tool you're using. Replace anything in [brackets] with your actual details. The more specific you are, the better the output. If the first response isn't quite right, say what needs to change - AI works best as a back-and-forth, not a one-shot.
Use case 01

Writing + Drafting

Bios, captions, emails, website copy - anything where you know what you want to say but the words aren't coming out right.

Prompt 1 - Instagram Bio
Write a 3-sentence Instagram bio for [what you do], for [who you help], based in [your location]. Tone: warm, real, no corporate language. Don't use the words "empower," "transform," "journey," or "hustle." End with a soft call to action pointing to my link in bio.

Tip: paste in your existing bio and say "rewrite this in a warmer, more human voice" if you're starting from something.

Prompt 2 - Instagram Caption
Write an Instagram caption about [topic or idea]. My audience is [describe who you're writing for]. The tone should feel like a text from a knowledgeable friend - warm, direct, no hype. Keep it under 150 words. End with a question or a soft invitation to book or reach out.

Tip: if it sounds too polished or salesy, say "make it sound less like marketing and more like a real person wrote it."

Prompt 3 - Email to a Potential Client
Help me write a short follow-up email to someone who enquired about [your service] but hasn't booked yet. Warm but not pushy. Remind them what the session covers, what the next step is, and how to book. Keep it to 3-4 short paragraphs.

Tip: paste in any details they shared in their enquiry so the email feels personal, not generic.

Use case 02

Planning + Mapping

Launch plans, content calendars, task prioritisation - when you have too many moving pieces and not enough order.

Prompt 1 - Simple Content Plan
I want to post on Instagram [X times per week] for the next [4 weeks / month]. My business is [describe briefly]. My content themes are [list 2-3 topics you regularly talk about]. Give me a simple posting plan - one idea per slot, enough to get started, not so detailed I feel overwhelmed.

Tip: once you have the plan, ask it to write the first 3 captions so you're not staring at a blank page.

Prompt 2 - Launch Prep Checklist
I'm launching [describe your offer] in [timeframe, e.g. 6 weeks]. I'm working solo with limited time. What are the essential things I need to have in place before I open it? Give me a simple checklist, no more than 12 items, in the order I should tackle them.

Tip: say "now help me figure out which 3 to do this week" once you have the list.

Prompt 3 - Sorting a Busy Brain
I have a lot of things I'm trying to do at once and I'm not sure what to focus on. Here's everything on my plate right now: [list everything - messy is fine]. Help me sort this into: what needs attention this week, what can wait, and what I should probably drop or delegate. I have about [X hours per week] to work on the business.

Tip: be honest about your hours. AI will prioritise differently if you have 5 hours vs 20.

Use case 03

AI as a Sounding Board

When you need to think out loud and there's no one at the kitchen table. AI won't have your instincts - but it can help you organise the noise.

Prompt 1 - Decision Support
I'm trying to decide between [option A] and [option B]. Here's the context: [explain the situation in a few sentences]. I'm not asking you to decide for me. I want you to help me think it through. Ask me 3-4 questions that would help me get clearer, and then reflect back what you're hearing.

Tip: when it reflects back, notice what lands as true and what doesn't. That's information.

Prompt 2 - Challenge My Thinking
I'm planning to [describe your plan or idea]. I want you to play devil's advocate. What are the things I might not have considered? Where could this go wrong? What assumptions am I making that I should test? Be direct - I want honest pushback, not reassurance.

Tip: useful when you've been sitting with an idea for a while and need a fresh angle.

Prompt 3 - Unsticking a Stuck Thing
I've been avoiding [describe the task or decision] for [how long]. I know I need to do it but I keep not doing it. Can you help me figure out what's actually in the way? I'll answer your questions honestly. Don't jump to solutions yet - help me understand the block first.

Tip: sometimes naming the real block out loud (even to AI) is enough to get moving.

Use case 04

Admin + Day-to-Day

The practical stuff that piles up when you're doing everything yourself.

Prompt 1 - Client Message or Response
Help me write a response to this client message: [paste the message]. I want to reply in a warm, professional tone. The outcome I'm hoping for is [e.g. they rebook / they understand the cancellation policy / they feel heard]. Keep it short - 3-4 sentences max.

Tip: always read it before sending and adjust anything that doesn't sound like you.

Prompt 2 - Policy or FAQ Draft
Help me write a simple [cancellation policy / booking policy / FAQs page] for my business. I offer [describe your services]. Key things to include: [list any specifics - e.g. 24hr cancellation window, no refunds on digital products, etc.]. Tone: clear and direct but not cold. Written for a solo service business, not a corporate.

Tip: have a quick read and check it covers everything - AI sometimes misses edge cases you'd know from experience.

Prompt 3 - Service Description for Website or Booking Page
Write a short description for my [service name] for my booking page or website. The service is [describe what happens, how long, what it's for]. It's for [who it's for]. Tone: warm and grounded, no hype, no woo-woo performance. Around 80-100 words.

Tip: if it sounds too generic, say "this sounds like it could be for anyone - make it more specific to [describe your niche]."

The soul-led check - always do this before you use anything AI writes. Does this still sound like me? Would I actually say this? Does it feel true - or just technically correct? If something feels off, it probably is. AI doesn't know your clients, your instincts, or your voice. It's a starting point. You're the editor.

Prompting gets better with practice.

The more context you give, the better the output. If a response isn't quite right, don't start over - just say what needs to change. The conversation approach works better than trying to get it right in one go.
